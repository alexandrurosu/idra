What this view is

Compliance asks the same question of every promise: what evidence shows it is kept, and where that evidence comes from (Chapter 3). This view traces each clause of the intent forward to its evidence, traces every function back to the clauses it serves, and says how far the evidence itself can be trusted: how obligations are assigned to clauses, and whether the same program gives the same evidence on another machine.

Every clause, forward to its evidence

"Link" says how strongly the clause is tied to its evidence: 9 direct, 1 by kind of check, 10 tested, 2 assumed, 1 gap. The levels, and why each clause has its level, are on the intent projection:

  • Direct: the clause has a formal statement, and the build checks that statement itself: a budget against a proved bound, an allowed set of effects, a boundary's write set, or a kind of check that is all the clause says.
  • By kind of check: obligations count for the clause because of their kind, not because of what they are about. They support it, but the evidence does not say which of them prove it.
  • Tested: a test shows it, on the runs recorded; nothing proves it.
  • Assumed: it is a stated assumption about something outside the program, checked by a test where one exists.
  • Gap: no evidence of its own.

Test, cost and assumed clauses are sorted by rule; the proof clauses are judged by hand, by reading each formal statement against the clause's text (tools/projections.py, LINK). "In part" means the link covers some of what the clause says, and the note says which.

A pill means the evidence exists; a dot that it does not. "Machine code" means every function that serves the clause was translation-validated: the image's own instructions were proved to do what the proved program does (5,676 pieces, 0 failed).

Every function, back to its intent

Chapter 4's provenance plane asks every node to answer "why does this exist?" All 146 functions name at least one clause: 109 name 1, 19 name 2, 12 name 3, 5 name 4, 1 name 5. No function exists without a reason in the intent; the system graph lists each one's clauses.

How obligations are assigned to clauses

The evidence records one clause for each obligation, chosen by the obligation's kind: the clause whose formal statement names that kind. So every precondition, postcondition, bound, invariant and lifetime obligation counts for C-5, including those that prove the TCP code's or the HTTP front end's contracts, and every arithmetic obligation counts for C-7. The per-clause counts on these pages are therefore counts by kind of check, not by what each obligation is about. The one exception made here is tenant isolation: its 12 preconditions are attributed to H-2 (see the trust projection).

Whether the evidence is reproducible

Each proof question is given a fixed amount of solver work, counted by Z3 itself, not a time limit, so the same program gives the same evidence on any machine (Appendix A, step 5). The limit is 400,000,000 units. The most any proved obligation used was 268,374,231 (67% of the limit); 1 used more than half and 32 more than a tenth. An obligation near the limit is the kind that a small change elsewhere can push over it, as happened once before (step 22).

How the checker reached each proof: flat 5,017, full 4,498, sliced 2,027, not recorded 368. Proofs are cached under a key that covers everything they depend on; this evidence is from a build on 2026-11-22 18:29:49 with idra-check-8 and Z3 5.4.1.

What the trace cannot show

  • That the formal statements say what the prose clauses mean. Nothing checks that link (Chapter 22).
  • Which obligations prove which part of a clause: the evidence assigns them by kind, as above. The link levels are a reading of the formal statements, not a record the build keeps; a build that recorded, for each obligation, the clause it is about would make them mechanical.
  • When the tests last ran against this build: they are linked by hand, and their runs are in the build log, not the evidence.
  • That a refused planted defect was found rather than merely not proved: 9 of the 21 were refused because the solver used up its work (see the intent projection).
